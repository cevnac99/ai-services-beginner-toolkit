# Quote and Proposal Generator

## The simple offer

Help a contractor or service provider turn approved discovery notes into a consistent first proposal draft while the owner controls prices, taxes, promises, and final terms.

## Who may buy it

Freelancers, agencies, consultants, trades, event providers, and local service businesses that rewrite similar proposals repeatedly.

## The repeated problem

Discovery notes are incomplete, proposal sections get missed, and staff spend time copying the same structure while still needing to verify every commercial term.

## Build this first

A fictional proposal draft containing:

- buyer goal and current problem;
- included deliverables;
- exclusions;
- assumptions and buyer responsibilities;
- timeline placeholder;
- price and tax placeholders;
- acceptance checks;
- unanswered questions.

## Five fictional test cases

1. Complete discovery notes for a small scoped service.
2. Missing timeline or decision maker.
3. Conflicting scope and budget notes.
4. Request outside the provider's service menu.
5. High-risk promise, legal term, tax treatment, or unusual guarantee.

## Copyable agent prompt

```text
Turn fictional discovery notes for a [SERVICE TYPE] into a proposal draft.

Use these sections: buyer goal, current problem, deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, buyer responsibilities, timeline placeholder, price placeholder, acceptance checks, and open questions.

Rules:
- do not invent prices, taxes, dates, legal terms, warranties, results, or availability;
- preserve uncertainty as an open question;
- flag conflicts instead of choosing one version;
- mark every commercial and legal term for owner review;
- do not send or publish the proposal.

Test complete, missing, conflicting, out-of-scope, and high-risk cases. Done when the draft is consistent and every unsupported term is visibly blocked.
```

## Questions for a possible buyer

- Which sections are repeated in every proposal?
- What information must be confirmed before quoting?
- Who approves price, tax, timeline, and legal language?
- Which requests usually create scope problems?
- What must the customer accept before work begins?

## What you can deliver

A proposal template, discovery intake checklist, exclusions library, acceptance-check section, and five-case test report.

## Human-controlled boundary

The business owner approves all prices, taxes, commitments, legal language, timelines, guarantees, and final delivery.
