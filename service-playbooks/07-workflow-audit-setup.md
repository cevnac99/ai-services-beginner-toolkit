# Workflow Audit and Setup

## The simple offer

Help a small business choose one repeated task worth improving before it buys tools or commits to a large automation project.

## Who may buy it

Small businesses that are curious about AI but cannot explain which workflow, result, access, or risk should come first.

## The repeated problem

The team starts with a tool name instead of a business problem, so projects become broad, expensive, or impossible to verify.

## Build this first

A fictional one-page workflow audit containing:

- who performs the task;
- trigger and current steps;
- inputs and systems involved;
- repeated delay or error;
- smallest useful output;
- human approval point;
- access and privacy risks;
- first demonstration recommendation.

## Five fictional test cases

1. Clear repeated task with a visible output.
2. Missing owner or success definition.
3. Two teams describe conflicting workflows.
4. One-off task with no useful automation case.
5. High-risk workflow involving sensitive data or important decisions.

## Copyable agent prompt

```text
Help me conduct a fictional AI workflow audit for a [BUSINESS TYPE].

For each candidate workflow identify: owner, trigger, current steps, inputs, repeated pain, useful output, human approval, access needed, privacy risk, and smallest safe demo.

Compare three candidate workflows and recommend only one using these filters: repeated, understandable, demonstrable with fictional data, reversible, and human-approved.

Do not recommend autonomous external actions, live credentials, sensitive-data access, or a large platform build. Preserve missing or conflicting information as questions.

Done when the recommendation fits on one page and the buyer can accept, reject, or correct every assumption.
```

## Questions for a possible buyer

- Which task repeats every week and frustrates the team?
- Who owns the task from start to finish?
- What visible result would make the task easier?
- Which data or systems are involved?
- What must always stay under human control?

## What you can deliver

A workflow map, scored opportunity list, risk boundary, one recommended demo, and a written next-step scope. This can be useful even when the correct recommendation is not to automate.

## Human-controlled boundary

The business decides priorities, grants access, accepts risks, approves production changes, and owns important decisions.

## Next move

Interview one operator for 20 minutes, draw the current steps, and ask them to correct the map before suggesting any tool.
