# Lead Follow-Up Assistant

## The simple offer

Help a local service business organize new inquiries and prepare consistent follow-up drafts while a staff member approves every message.

## Who may buy it

Plumbers, cleaners, landscapers, repair companies, photographers, clinics with non-medical inquiries, and other businesses that receive leads through forms, email, or messages.

## The repeated problem

New inquiries arrive with different details. Staff reply late, forget a question, or cannot see which lead needs the next action.

## Build this first

A fictional inbox table that turns each inquiry into:

- contact and requested service;
- known and missing details;
- urgency flag based only on approved rules;
- draft reply;
- next follow-up date;
- `needs human review` status.

Do not connect a real inbox or send anything.

## Five fictional test cases

1. Complete request with service, location, and preferred time.
2. Missing location and contact method.
3. Conflicting dates in the same inquiry.
4. Request outside the business service area.
5. Complaint, emergency, or sensitive request requiring a person.

## Copyable agent prompt

```text
Help me build a fictional-data lead follow-up demonstration for a local [BUSINESS TYPE].

Create five sample inquiries: complete, missing information, conflicting information, out of scope, and human handoff.

For each inquiry produce: summary, missing details, approved urgency label, draft reply, next action, and review status.

Rules:
- never send a message;
- never invent prices, availability, policies, or customer facts;
- flag uncertainty instead of guessing;
- complaints, emergencies, refunds, legal issues, and sensitive cases go to a person;
- use only fictional names and data.

Done when all five cases produce a clear result or fail safely and I can explain the workflow to a business owner in two minutes.
```

## Questions for a possible buyer

- Where do inquiries arrive today?
- Which details are required before anyone can reply?
- What makes a lead urgent, and who approved that rule?
- Which requests should never receive an automatic draft?
- What does a useful daily follow-up list look like?

## What you can deliver

A workflow map, five tested fictional cases, draft template set, review checklist, and a short demonstration. Live setup is a later scope only after access, privacy, and approval rules are agreed in writing.

## Human-controlled boundary

People approve messages, prices, availability, lead quality, complaints, emergencies, and any customer-facing action.

## Next move

Build the five cases, record a two-minute walkthrough, and ask one local business owner which column or decision is missing.
